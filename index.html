Exploring the chemistry of cosmic ices under interstellar conditions

I am a postdoctoral researcher at HUN-REN Institute for Nuclear Research (ATOMKI), Debrecen, Hungary. My work focuses on ultra-high-vacuum cryogenic experiments, infrared and ultraviolet spectroscopy, and the energetic processing of astrophysical ices to understand the formation of complex organic and aromatic molecules in space.

About

My research investigates the behavior of molecular ices under low-temperature astrophysical conditions relevant to the interstellar medium, icy moons, planetary environments and comets. I use ultra high vacuum, cryogenic systems together with FTIR, UV, and VUV spectroscopy to study ice morphology, molecular interactions, and irradiation-driven chemical evolution.

Research Highlights

Laboratory Simulation of Astrochemical Conditions

Simulating low-temperature astrophysical environments [ultra high vacuum and cryogenic conditions (~10 K)] in the laboratory and preparing molecular ice analogues.

Ice Morphology & Phase Transitions

Investigating the morphology, structural evolution, and phase transitions of molecular ices using FTIR and VUV spectroscopy under controlled laboratory conditions.

Energetic Processing & Ice Chemistry

Processing molecular ices with photons, electrons and charged particles and studying the resulting chemical evolution, reaction pathways, and complex molecule formation using FTIR and synchrotron-based VUV spectroscopy.
